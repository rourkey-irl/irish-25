How to Play

Irish 25

Five cards each, one suit rules them all, and the first player to 25 points wins — sometimes mid-trick.

Setup

Five cards, one flip

You play against up to five AI opponents. Everyone is dealt 5 cards from a standard 52-card deck — the rest of the deck sits aside, except for one card turned face-up.

The suit of that turned-up card is trumps for the round. It outranks every other suit, no matter what's led. The player to the dealer's left leads the first trick.

The Trump Suit

Not A–K. A special ladder.

Trumps don't rank the way you'd expect. Three cards always sit at the very top, regardless of suit — then the rest fall in by colour.

1st 5 ♥ The 5 of trumps — the single highest card in the game.
2nd J ♥ The Jack of trumps.
3rd A ♥ The Ace of Hearts — always a trump, even when hearts isn't the trump suit.
4th A ♥ The Ace of the trump suit (only a separate card when trumps aren't hearts).

Then the remaining trumps follow colour order — red and black suits run in opposite directions:

Red trumps (♥ ♦)

KQ109876432

Black trumps (♣ ♠)

KQ234678910

Each row reads highest → lowest. (The 5, J and A are already placed above, so they don't repeat here.)

Non-Trump Suits

The Ace is in two different places

When a suit isn't trumps, red and black suits rank differently from each other — and from what you might expect.

Red (♥ ♦), not trump

KQJ1098765432A

The Ace is the weakest card.

Black (♣ ♠), not trump

KQJA2345678910

The Ace sits just under the Jack — stronger than 2–10.

The Special Rule

Reneging

Normally, if a trump is led and you hold one, you must play it. But the three untouchable top trumps come with a privilege: you may refuse to play them — "renege" — and throw a different card instead.

5 ♥ J ♥ A ♥ — the only cards that can renege

Two conditions have to hold at once:

  • Every trump in your hand is one of those three cards (no ordinary trump hiding alongside them), and
  • each one you hold already outranks the card that was led.

If either condition fails — say you also hold a plain trump — you must play a trump as usual (any trump you hold is legal, including a top one if you choose).

Example: Diamonds are trumps. Someone leads the 9♦. If your only trump is the A♥, you may renege — the ace already beats a 9, so you're allowed to save it. But if you also held the 7♦, you'd have to play a trump.

Before Play Begins

Robbing the Pack

Whoever holds the Ace of the trump suit may "rob": trade it for the turned-up trump card, discarding one card from hand to make room. This can only happen once, before the first trick is played.

A ♦ In your hand
⇔
9 ♦ Turned up card

You end up holding the (usually stronger) turned-up trump instead of your ace, having discarded a card you no longer need.

A ♦ Ace to the face — if the turned-up card itself is the Ace of trumps, nobody holds it in hand. The dealer robs it instead, trading it in for a discard exactly as above.

Scoring & Winning

Race to 25

  • Each trick is worth 5 points, awarded the instant it's won.
  • The winner of a trick leads the next one.
  • The moment any player reaches 25 points, the game ends — even if it's only the second trick of the round.
  • If nobody has reached 25 after all 5 tricks, a new round is dealt and trumps are set again.

Quick Reference

Glossary

Trick
One card from each player; highest wins and takes 5 points.
Trump
The suit set by the turned-up card; beats every other suit.
Lead
The first card played to a trick; sets the suit others must follow.
Follow suit
Play the same suit that was led, if you can.
Renege
Legally withhold the 5, J, or A♥ from a trump lead it would've beaten.
Rob
Swap your Ace of trumps for the turned-up trump card before play starts.
